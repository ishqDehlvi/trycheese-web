**Is film photography making a comeback?** On 2026's numbers, yes, with one honest caveat. No film maker publishes how many rolls of still film it sells. But the companies that make film are growing, investing in new production, and keeping prices roughly flat.

Kodak's results on 3 November are the latest sign. The unit that includes its motion picture and industrial film grew revenue 21% year on year. Here's what the numbers say, and what they don't.

## The evidence at a glance

| Signal | What 2026 shows |
| --- | --- |
| Kodak | Advanced Materials & Chemicals revenue up 21% in Q3, to $99 million |
| Motion picture film | Revenue up 36% over two years, according to a Kodak executive |
| Fujifilm Instax | A fifth straight record year, and new film lines on the way |
| Film prices | Up 2.5% on average from January to July, after six flat months |
| New products | New Kodak film names, a new Instax film, a new Polaroid camera |

## What Kodak's results do and don't tell you

Kodak's Q3 2026 release is upbeat. Revenue rose 3% to $276 million, net income rose from $13 million to $21 million, and Advanced Materials & Chemicals (AM&C) went from $82 million to $99 million. CEO Jim Continenza called AM&C the company's "fastest growing, most profitable unit". The unit's Operational EBITDA, Kodak's preferred profit measure, rose from $16 million to $21 million.

That follows a strong second quarter, when, as PetaPixel reported, AM&C revenue rose by $30 million to $105 million.

Here's the caveat. AM&C isn't just camera film. PetaPixel, citing CineD, lists industrial film and chemicals, motion picture film, pharmaceuticals, advanced materials and licensing inside it, and Kodak doesn't break the pieces out. The one film figure we have came from a Kodak executive on a factory tour: motion picture film revenue up 36% over two years.

Two more details matter for anyone who shoots film:

- **Silver costs bite.** Kodak says higher aluminium and silver prices offset some of its gains. Film is made with silver, so that's a pressure on future prices.
- **Kodak sells its own film now.** Eastman Kodak has brought distribution of its still films in-house, which is why [Portra is now sold as Ektacolor Pro](/blog/ektacolor-pro-vs-portra).

## Instant film is the clearest growth story

If you want an unambiguous number, it's Fujifilm's. The company says Instax sales reached 200 billion yen in FY2025, its fifth consecutive record year since FY2021. On 20 October it announced about 3 billion yen for new Instax film lines in Japan, raising capacity by about 20% by spring 2028, and by about 70% compared with FY2022 once all its recent investments are running.

Fujifilm is still adding new emulsions, too. A week later it announced SQUARE Soft Haze, a film with a new emulsion for soft light and warm tones, alongside the instax SQUARE 2 camera. Polaroid, meanwhile, describes its Netherlands plant as the world's last Polaroid film factory. If you're choosing between the two systems, here's [how Instax and Polaroid compare](/blog/instax-vs-polaroid-film).

## Prices: stable, not cheap

A comeback that priced everyone out wouldn't last. The good news is that film prices have been steady. Analog.Cafe's Film Price Report tracks 37 film products across 13 major stores, and found prices up 2.5% on average from January to July 2026, after six months with no change.

The averages hide a lot of movement:

- **Cheaper:** Kodak Ektapan 400, the renamed T-Max, fell 4.9%, and Kodak Ultramax 400 fell 2.6%.
- **Dearer:** Kodak Kodacolor 200, formerly ColorPlus, rose 10.5%, and Ilford HP5 Plus rose 7.6%.
- **By region:** Film got 6% more expensive in Europe and Canada, and 1% cheaper in Hong Kong.

Analog.Cafe also makes a neat point about history. A 24-exposure roll of Kodak Gold 100 that cost $4.60 in the 1990s would be about $11.40 in today's money. A 36-exposure roll of Gold 200 averages $10.34 in 2026.

## Even digital is copying film

The other sign of film's pull is how hard digital is working to imitate it. Fujifilm's X half, a digital camera, now offers a Film Camera Mode with 12- and 24-frame rolls. The iPhone 18 Pro adds a Film texture and a Grain control to Photographic Styles. Google's Snapseed brought [a free film camera to Android](/blog/snapseed-camera-android) this year.

None of that is film. But it shows what people want from it: a look, a limit, and a little wait. We break down the look itself in [what makes a photo look like film](/blog/what-makes-photos-look-like-film).

## If you're thinking of starting

A few practical pointers, based on the numbers above:

1. **Instant is the easiest start.** It's more per shot, but there's no lab and no scanning.
2. **35mm colour is cheaper per frame.** Kodak Gold and Ultramax remain the budget workhorses.
3. **Black and white is forgiving.** Ilford suggests HP5 Plus as a first film, as we cover in [the black and white film look](/blog/black-and-white-film-look).
4. **Check prices before you buy.** They vary more by shop and region than by year.
